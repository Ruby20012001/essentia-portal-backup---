What This Role Asks of You

The bridge between Monica's intent and the developer's code

What you do every day during the build

  • Translate each section of the 39-section Portal Brief into specific, testable sprint tasks — written so plainly that a developer can act without asking you a follow-up question
  • Run the 15-minute daily standup with the developer team — what was done, what is being built today, what is blocked
  • Write the 3-line morning brief to Monica — she reads it in under 5 minutes. This is the only routine touchpoint she needs from you.
  • Review every screen before Monica sees it — you are the filter. Nothing reaches her that doesn't already match the brief.
  • Maintain the sprint tracker — every task has an owner, a due date, and a pass/fail criterion. If it isn't in the tracker, it didn't happen.
  • Chair the Monday 60-minute build meeting — your agenda, your pace, closed exactly on time
  • Escalate any blocker to Monica within 24 hours — always with a proposed solution attached, never just a problem

What you do after the portal goes live

  • Manage the ongoing sprint cycle — new features get added to the brief and built in the order you set
  • Be the first point of contact for any of the 490 staff who cannot find what they need in the portal
  • Run the monthly portal health review with Deepak Ji — adoption rates, data quality flags, any auto-pilot event that misfired
  • Write the specifications for every new feature Monica wants added
  • Train the senior layer of staff on the portal — they then train their own teams. You are not training 490 people. You are training perhaps 25.
Why you, specifically

This role does not need a developer. It needs someone who can sit with 39 dense sections, hold the structure in their head, and not lose the thread. That is the deep work Monica picked you for.

Time commitment

Full-time during the build (5 days/week, ~105 days). Settles to 2-3 days/week once the portal is live and stable.

The one thing that will test you most

Holding the line on the brief. Engineers will want to simplify things that matter. Your job is to know which simplifications are fine and which ones quietly break the system later.
